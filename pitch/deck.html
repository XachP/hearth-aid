The problem: transparency can hurt the people aid is for

  • Cash grants are sized by household size, disability, illness, protection status.
  • On a transparent chain, a grant amount is sensitive personal data, published forever, readable by neighbours, landlords, armed groups and data brokers.
  • The ICRC data-protection handbook and GDPR Art. 9 say: don't. "Do No Harm."
  • Yet donors need proof: no double-dipping, no ghost households, books that reconcile.

Today agencies must choose: transparency or safety.

Hearth: each party sees exactly what it needs

WhoSees
👁 Public / armed groupFunds raised, household count, grants per round. No amounts.
🔑 Each householdIts own grant plus a private note, opened with its own key
📜 Donor's auditorEvery amount + treasury, reconciled to the unit, with no spending power
🏘 Another householdOnly its own grant
🦹 Double-dipperRejected on-chain via salted-hash enrolment, with no PII stored

Built for the field, not the demo

  • Salted-hash enrolment: raw IDs are refused; each tag is unique forever, even after offboarding.
  • Lost phone: replace_wallet keeps the household's place and paid status, so a new wallet never creates a second entitlement.
  • Atomic rounds: up to 64 grants in one tx. One bad grant rejects all of them. The only event is a count.
  • Can't leak by mistake: revealed grants are rejected. The auditor key is locked on the token for its lifetime.
  • Proof to a landlord: a family can prove one grant and reveal nothing else.

Path to a real pilot

  • Voucher mode: spend at vetted merchants; the auditor reconciles redemptions.
  • Governance: multi-sig operator; per-donor auditor keys with rotation.
  • Reach: a USSD/SMS front end for feature phones.
  • Pilot: one local NGO, one district, transfer values sized by the Sphere minimum standards.

Honest limits are in THREAT_MODEL.md: the chain proves uniqueness, not that a person is real, so field verification stays essential.
